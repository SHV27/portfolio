The 34,335 duplicate rows are the dangerous one. They are not corrupt or malformed — they are perfectly valid-looking transaction lines that happen to appear twice. Summed naively they inflate revenue, and no error is raised, no warning appears, and the total still looks entirely reasonable. METHOD
How you can know these numbers are right, without taking anyone's word for it.

Four mechanisms, in descending order of how much work they save you. The first three are structural — they make a wrong number impossible rather than unlikely.

Money is never a floating-point number
src/engine/money.ts

Every amount is an integer count of ten-thousandths of a currency unit. The conversion happens once, at ingest, and any value that will not round-trip exactly is refused rather than rounded. Constructing a money value from a fraction throws, as does one beyond the range where integer arithmetic stays exact. So "balances to the penny" is arithmetic, not a slogan. On the bundled sample the measured round-trip error is 0.0 and the grand total sits 46,700× inside JavaScript's safe-integer limit.

Every explanation must reconcile, or it is refused
src/engine/run.ts · preflight()

Before any exhibit renders, the engine re-adds its own ledger and compares it to the observed movement. Not within a tolerance — exactly. If the terms do not sum to the total, the finding is replaced by a refusal that names the gap. This runs at render time in your browser, on the finding in front of you.

The model is never shown the data, and cannot type a digit
api/narrate.ts · src/narrate/slots.ts

The language model receives a list of placeholder names — no rows, no figures, no file contents, not even for the public sample. It returns a sentence of {{placeholders}} which this browser fills from its own arithmetic. Output containing a digit outside a placeholder is rejected automatically. An invented figure is not unlikely here; it has no route to the screen.

The invariants are proved over generated data, and the tests are proved to fail
src/engine/engine.test.ts · src/narrate/slots.test.ts

Property-based tests build thousands of random datasets and assert that the decomposition reconciles on every one. Then — because a test that cannot fail proves nothing — three deliberate bugs were introduced into the engine and each was confirmed to break the suite: computing the mix term independently, dropping the residual, and disabling the period gate.

THE LIMITS
What this product cannot do, and where it could still mislead you.

Stated plainly, because a tool that claims no weaknesses is telling you its first lie. Of the seven major "ask your data" products surveyed while building this, only two publish an accuracy figure at all — Snowflake on 150 internal questions and Databricks on 28 — and neither has been independently replicated.

It explains WHAT moved. It does not know WHY.

The bridge and the ledger are arithmetic: they say where the money went, exactly. They cannot tell you that a competitor opened nearby or that a supplier failed. Causal claims need an experiment or a natural one, and this product deliberately does not pretend otherwise — automatic causal verdicts were considered and cut, because their assumptions cannot be tested from the data alone.

The AI wording can attach a right number to the wrong subject.

Across 18 live generations the model never wrote a digit — it structurally cannot. But roughly one paragraph in six placed a correct figure against the wrong noun, for instance giving one country a share that belonged to all eight. That is why AI wording is off by default, behind a labelled button, and why the deterministic exhibits above it are the authoritative ones.

'Reconciles exactly' is a statement about arithmetic, not about your data being right.

If the source file is wrong, this will faithfully explain a wrong number. That is why the data-quality exhibit runs first and why it is stated before any finding rather than after.

Only one comparison shape is supported: two periods of one money metric.

No forecasting, no cohort retention, no multi-metric models. Each was considered and cut rather than shipped shallow. A narrow tool that is right beats a broad one that is plausible.

The sample ledger is real, but it is from 2009–2011.

It is a genuine UK retailer's transaction file, chosen because it is real, openly licensed, and — usefully — full of the exact defects this product exists to catch. It is not current, and the interface says so on every exhibit rather than quietly implying freshness.

Findings describe the periods compared, not a trend.

Two periods are two points. A movement between them is not a direction of travel, and nothing here should be read as one.

It has not been evaluated by a real CEO.

It has been driven end to end in a real browser at desktop and phone widths, checked for console errors and layout failures, and audited against published research on what executives actually reject in analytics tools. That is the strongest evaluation available without access to executives, and it is not the same thing as one. Stated so nobody has to guess.
